# News Article Analyzer

A live NLP tool that takes any news article — pasted text or a URL — and breaks it down into a summary, sentiment reading, and topic classification, in one pass.

**Live app:** http://18.222.173.178:8501

## What it does

Paste a full article or drop in a URL, and the app returns:

- **Summary** — a condensed version of the article generated by DistilBART, with an adjustable target length
- **Sentiment** — Positive / Negative / Neutral, with a choice between two models: RoBERTa (fine-tuned for social/news text, higher accuracy) or VADER (rule-based, much faster)
- **Topic** — one of 8 news categories (e.g. Court & Justice, International Politics, Business & Education), assigned by an NMF topic model trained on article text, with a full weight breakdown across all 8 topics available on request

Every result comes with its own confidence score, and the sentiment/topic breakdowns are available in expandable detail views rather than just a single label — useful for understanding *why* the model landed where it did, not just the headline answer.

## Why it matters

Most "paste an article, get a summary" demos stop at one model doing one job. This pipeline runs three distinct NLP tasks — abstractive summarization, sentiment classification, and unsupervised topic modeling — over the same input and reconciles them into one report, closer to how an actual editorial or media-monitoring tool would need to work.

It's also fully self-contained: every model runs locally (no external LLM API, no per-request cost), which means the app has flat, predictable resource usage instead of usage-based billing — a deliberate choice for a project meant to sit on a free-tier server indefinitely.

## Tech stack

- **DistilBART-CNN-12-6** (Hugging Face) — abstractive summarization
- **Cardiff NLP Twitter-RoBERTa** — sentiment classification
- **VADER** — lightweight rule-based sentiment, offered as a faster alternative
- **NMF (scikit-learn)** — unsupervised topic modeling over TF-IDF features, 8 topics
- **newspaper3k** — article extraction directly from a pasted URL
- **Streamlit** — UI and app hosting

## Limitations

- The NMF topic model's 8 categories were defined by the training data it saw and won't generalize perfectly to every subject an article could cover.
- Sentiment models are tuned on general/social text, not specifically news journalism, so tone (e.g. dry factual reporting on a negative event) can occasionally skew the label.
- Summarization truncates very long articles (first ~3,000 characters) rather than processing the full text, to keep response times reasonable on CPU-only hosting.
